Getting started / Introduction

Introduction

The design system for ctrl.rodeo/applications — the app that moves a person from application to Agape housemate. One screen of rows carries the whole funnel, so nearly every rule here is about earning the right to put something on a row.

Apply→ Inbox→ Candidates→ Openings→ Screening→ Visit · decision

Notes

Dark-first, Inter/system sans, rounded rectangles for actions and pills for state. Blue stages are automated by the app; grey ones are human-run (for now). Source of truth for behavior: docs/ux/recruiting-row-states.md in the repo; this site is the articulated why.

Principles

Every rule below was earned by deleting something that violated it.

·
One primary action per rowThe right rail answers "whose move is it?" with exactly one button. If a row needs two buttons, the state machine is wrong — demote one to the context tier or an icon.
·
Two tiers: action, then contextPrimary on top; timestamps, secondary links, and who/when live in a quiet caption beneath.
·
Name the goal, not the transport"Ask for coverage," never "Post to Discord" as a goal label. Mechanism belongs in subcopy.
·
Never explain the UINo "tap a time to book it." Outcome copy is welcome: "Invites will be sent to both of you, with an email introduction."
·
Never be redundantOne quote plus one interpretation line. If a badge says it, the subline doesn't repeat it.
·
Select, then confirmAnything that commits takes two taps: a selection state, then a labeled confirm. Never a native dialog in a polished flow.
·
Whitespace over chromeOverflow menus are borderless ⋮ until hover. Hint text and toolbars get removed before a divider gets added.
·
Dates have to line upA candidate appears under a listing only when their window genuinely overlaps it. "Flexible" must be their word, not our guess.

Decision log

Why it is the way it is — dated, so future arguments have receipts.

Jul 30
Settings shipped, and admin is a Discord channel.

Six sections rendered from a field schema, so adding a knob is one object literal. Admin = whoever can see #recruiting-automation — the channel that already gets the audit trail decides who changes the automation — reconciled into recruit_admins, which is what RLS consults. Four literals became real settings (follow-up staleness, move-in flex, the two trial offsets); four more route through setting() with schema defaults but stay out of the UI. See Settings.

Jul 30
An open stretch asks before it shows a form.

The gap drawer used to stack a listing button on top of a full stay form. Now it offers two inset tiles — Create a listing / Add an occupant — and becomes the flow you pick, with one way back. The listing form moved into the drawer so the flow never leaves the sidebar it started in.

Jul 30
Nothing has a Save button unless it creates, confirms, or destroys.

Editing an existing record writes on change — text on blur, dates and selects the instant they settle — and a quiet line says so, flashing "Saved" when a write lands. Save survived only where it means something: Add stay and Create listing (a record that doesn't exist can't autosave), Welcome them in (a confirmation), and the destructive tiles. The app says this in no words at all — the line is empty until a write lands, then reads “Saved” for a moment.

Jul 30
Direction A won: exits are inset tiles, and the footer has no hairlines.

Each exit is its own filled surface with radius and a 6px gap; the commit row dropped its border-top too. The rules were separating rows that belonged together. The __alt stays outlined — that's now what distinguishes a forward path from a way out.

Jul 30
The exit rows' hairlines were up for replacement.

Full-bleed rules between exits read as a 2015 settings list — the rule was separating things that should have been grouped. Five directions drawn side by side (inset tiles · inset-grouped · space only · danger zone · overflow), with a recommendation: group them in one container with no internal rules, and promote only the genuinely irreversible to a tinted danger block. Undecided — see Exit rows — directions.

Jul 30
Settings gets the rail's vocabulary, and a schema instead of a page.

The rail footer was the only place a setting could go, so most settings never became settings — the house's recruiting behavior lives in literals (3 quiet days, ±1 flexible month, +1/−1 trial milestones) that need a deploy to change. The concept: six sections named You · House · Funnel · Automations · Connections · Data, rendered from a field schema where scope picks the store and the schema default is the code default. Concept only — see Settings.

Jul 30
Inbox is Applicants, and Screening left the rail.

"Inbox" described a queue; "Applicants" names what's in it. Screening was a rail entry nobody navigated to — the work happens on a person's row and in Discord — so the view stays routable for deep links and left the rail.

Jul 30
The design system got a name: Sassy.

"The design system" was a description, not a name, so nothing in a review could refer to it without a paragraph of context. Sassy is the whole thing — the global CTRL layer and every product system under it, this one included.

Jul 30
Sidebar CTAs became three tiers instead of one flex row.

The stay drawer's footer had four actions crammed right-aligned: two red underlined links (both wrapping onto two lines), a text Cancel, and the primary. Commit, dismiss, and the ways out are now separate tiers, and anything destructive leaves the commit row entirely. See Sidebar CTAs.

Jul 30
Occupancy bars are exact, and empty stretches are unlabelled.

The break between back-to-back stays comes off each bar's right edge only, so a left edge still lands on its start date to the pixel. Month gridlines moved to one overlay on the header's own grid — the per-lane repeating gradient drifted a pixel or two per month. Red stretches dropped their "Open" label: the color already says it, and tapping says the dates.

Jul 29
The single decider came back — with three options and a required comment.

Quorum stalled: 58 applications sat waiting for a third vote that never came. One read now decides (not a fit / needs input / move forward), and the comment is the accountability that the tally used to pretend to be. "Needs input" is the honest version of a hung vote.

Jul 22
Collective votes replaced the single decider.

Culture fit is the house's call; the app's job is quorum, blindness until you've voted, and a hard veto. Superseded Jul 29.

Jul 22
AI suggestions replaced by deterministic placement.

A candidate is in every listing they qualify for, by rule. Confidence percentages were deleted — dates either line up or they don't.

Jul 22
Discord posts are manual-with-preview, for now.

A human sees the exact message before the house does. Auto-posting is a cutover we'll earn, not a default.

Jul 22
Pick-a-time beat the Discord post as the schedule primary.

Self-serve booking is the fast path; coverage is the fallback, so it reads as one ("Ask for coverage").

Jul 22
The rail became two tiers with a fixed-width column.

One primary, quiet context beneath, every edge aligned. The alternative — badges, timestamps, and two buttons jostling inline — is where the old design died.

Jul 22
Accessible-dark palette softened to friendly mid-tones.

Strict AA darks read as heavy. The system trades measured contrast for warmth at semibold sizes — a deliberate, documented exception.

Jul 27
The outstanding list shipped in one pass.

Phase B (new-application pings + the update-email tray), draft listings from occupancy gaps, awaiting-claim and give-decision states, follow-up staleness, visit drafts, the flexible ±1 month dial, and auto-posting as a settings toggle rather than a deploy.

Jul 22
The profile footer joined the state machine.

The lime/red decision pills were the last pre-v3 survivors; the footer now shows the same primary as the person's row.

Sidebar CTAs

A drawer footer is three tiers deep, ordered by consequence — with no hairlines and, on an existing record, no Save at all.

Changes save as you go

…and on a new record, where autosave has nothing to write to:

The tiers

__commitOne filled primary — and only when it's earned. A new record, a confirmation, or something destructive. Never a plain Save. __quietDismiss. No fill, no border, beside the primary. Cancel is not a decision, so it does not look like one. __flagWhat replaces Save. On an existing record: "Changes save as you go", flashing "Saved" when a write lands. The absence of a button should never read as "nothing happened". __exitThe ways out. Inset tiles — filled surface, radius, 6px gap, no rules. Label over hint, glyph right. Destructive ones take --danger. __altOptional second path. Outlined, never filled — that's what separates a forward move from a way out now that exits are filled.

Notes

  • No hairlines. A rule between two things that belong together separates what should be grouped. Exits are tiles; the commit row lost its border-top too. (Direction A — see the five directions.)
  • No Save on an existing record. Fields write on change: text on blur, dates and selects the instant they settle. An explicit commit survives only for create, confirm, and destroy.
  • Autosave repaints only what changed — one timeline lane and the drawer subtitle. A whole-view render rebuilds the form and throws away the caret.
  • Anything that removes, ends, or re-routes a record leaves the commit row. A 340px sidebar cannot keep four targets honest side by side — this is exactly how the stay drawer ended up with two red underlined links wrapping onto two lines each.
  • Exits never underline. Weight and color carry them; see Voice and the transparency-not-decoration rule.
  • Every exit and alt carries a hint. A red label is never the only explanation of what a button does.
  • Full-width rows mean labels never truncate, so the copy can stay plain ("Mark leaving") instead of compressed ("Mark leaving — list room").
  • Under 480px the hint drops to its own line rather than squeezing the label into an ellipsis.

Exit rows — directions A won

Tier 3 currently separates its rows with full-bleed hairlines. That's a 2015 settings-list move: the rule is doing work that surface, radius, and space should do. Five directions, same content, same 340px frame.

A · Inset tilesLinear, Vercel, Raycast
B · Inset-grouped listiOS 18, macOS Settings
C · Space onlyNotion, Linear menus
D · Danger zoneGitHub, Stripe, Vercel
Remove stay deletes it from the timeline
E · Collapse into overflowthis app's own kebab

Trade-offs

DirectionWhat it buysWhat it costs
A · Inset tiles No rules at all — each exit is a discrete object you can hit. Reads the most current of the five. Hover state has somewhere to live. Two filled tiles under a filled primary is a lot of surface in a 340px column; risks looking like three competing buttons.
B · Inset-grouped The exits read as one thing — "the ways out" — rather than unrelated links. No internal hairlines; the container edge does the grouping. Scales to 4+ exits without striping. Still a bordered box; if a section only ever has one exit, the container is overhead.
C · Space only Lightest possible. Nothing but type, a glyph, and whitespace — the most honest reading of "show, don't decorate". Weakest affordance: without a rule or a surface these can read as static text until hovered. Touch targets get ambiguous.
D · Danger zone Consequence is encoded in the container, not just the label color. Unmistakable, and the current standard for irreversible actions. Heavy for two items. The tint competes with the primary for attention — wrong emphasis when Save is the common case.
E · Overflow Footer collapses to commit + dismiss. Reuses a component the app already ships. Zero new visual language. Hides the exits behind a click, and hints don't survive a menu row — the thing that made "Mark leaving" legible goes away.

Recommendation

B, with D reserved for the genuinely irreversible.

B kills the hairlines and makes the exits one grouped object instead of a striped list, which is the actual complaint — the rules were separating things that should have been grouped. It keeps every hint inline, holds up at one exit or five, and needs no new tokens. Then, when an action can't be undone (deleting a listing with candidates attached, disconnecting Gmail), promote just that one to D's tinted block — the tint means "this one is different," which only works if it's rare.

  • Not A — under a filled accent pill, two filled tiles make three button-ish objects in a 340px column. A is the better choice on a wide surface; Settings sections are where it would earn its keep.
  • Not C — the app's rows already lean on hover to reveal affordance, and losing both the rule and the surface leaves destructive actions looking like captions.
  • Not E — the hints are the point. "Mark leaving" without "sets a move-out date and lists the room" is exactly the copy problem this pattern was built to fix.
  • B is drawn without a trailing chevron on purpose — the whole row is the target, and a chevron implies a further screen that doesn't exist.

Structure

  • The sections are the rail's words. You · House · Funnel · Automations · Connections · Data. The app already teaches House and Funnel; settings shouldn't teach a second taxonomy. If a new setting has no obvious home, the setting is confused — not the taxonomy.
  • Rendered from a schema, not hand-written. Each field declares { key, label, hint, type, scope, default, min, max, unit }; four types (bool · number · text · enum) cover every knob in the app. Adding a setting is appending an object.
  • scope picks the store — house → recruit_settings, profile → recruit_profiles, local → localStorage. Callers use setting(key) and never learn where a value lives.
  • The schema default is the code default. setting('followup_stale_days') returns 3 until someone changes it, so exposing a knob needs no migration and no seed — and today's magic numbers (3 days, ±1 month, +3 months) become one named thing each.
  • Automations and connections are their own row types, not fields: what it does, how often, when it last ran, whether it's on. Those are the four things you want when something didn't happen.
  • Save behavior follows the type. Toggles and enums autosave with an inline tick; numbers and text take an explicit commit. The schema knows the type, so the renderer enforces it — no per-field config.
  • Section footers are Sidebar CTAs — one commit, and “reset this section” as an exit row with a hint.

Who can change what

Admin is derived from Discord: whoever can see #recruiting-automation. Not a role the app maintains — the channel that already receives the automation audit trail decides who gets to change the automation. Lose channel access, lose admin, on the next verify.

Everyone reads everything. Non-admins see the same values, disabled, with one line saying so — read access was never the thing worth protecting. Every house-wide setting carries "Ian changed this 3 days ago"; RLS is the actual wall, and the disabled control is just courtesy.

Notes

A full view at ?view=settings, not a modal — every section is deep-linkable, so a toast or a Discord audit post can point at the exact knob. The rail footer dropped to identity · Sign out; theme moved to You, Gmail to Connections, CSV export to Data, and food/dues left the room drawer (a house-wide cost has no business inside one room) for House.

Automations report, they don't rule. Each row says what it does, how often, when it last ran, and whether it's on — the four things you want when something didn't happen. Cadence is pg_cron's, not this page's; a half-working toggle would be worse than saying where it lives.

Notes

Stage machine

The visual system sits on deterministic, server-side rules — never client opinion.

1
Inbox → CandidatesOne review decides, comment required. "Move forward" → Candidates; "Not a fit" → Archive with an update email owed; "Needs input" holds them in the Inbox. The most recent decisive verdict wins, so re-reviewing is how you change your mind. Budget under $1,500/mo auto-archives at load.
2
Candidates → OpeningsAuto-placement into every open listing whose dates genuinely line up (track match, budget covers rent, window overlap — no padding). Removals are tombstoned; the sweep never re-adds.
3
Openings → ScreeningAvailability parsed from replies (thread-matched, any sender address); calls booked in-app, via a Discord claim, or picked up automatically from the shared calendar.
4
Recruiter-confirmed dates winA confirmed window replaces parsed text everywhere — display, filters, placement — and is exact: no "flexible" escape hatch.
5
The update email is offered, not owed by forceArchiving queues an update and surfaces "Write their update" beside "Skip the email" — a fixed community template plus one paragraph drawn from their own survey answers, and an optional newsletter link. Outreach is closed for archived people at every layer, including a 409 from the send endpoint: we never invite someone to book a time after turning them down.
