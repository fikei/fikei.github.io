Invoy's Weekly Plans

A weekly coaching system for weight management. Members plan their week, check in daily, review progress, and adjust — with coach support when the data says they need it.

Role
Lead product designer (Senior PM)
Company
Invoy Health
Scope
Concept Design, Feature UI, Design System
Status
Shipped to production, ~CTR M3

Framing

Most health apps treat each screen as a standalone moment. You check in today, but the app doesn't connect that to what you planned this week or what happened yesterday. The member has to hold the context in their head.

Invoy needed a loop, not a list of features. Weekly planning should shape daily actions. Daily data should trigger coaching. Weekly review should change next week's plan.

Motivation: Structure Direction Me
  • Decision fatigue is the enemy. Defaults to the easiest option without a clear plan. Needs to know what today looks like.
→ Week Planning, Daily Focus, Base Plan
Motivation: Support "Don't Let Me Do This Alone"
  • Knows their triggers. Struggles when unseen. Previous attempts failed not because the plan was wrong but because they were doing it alone.
→ Rebound flow, Coach triage, Score Drop CTA
Motivation: Understanding "Help Me Understand Why"
  • Asks "why" before "what." Won't follow rules they don't understand. The moment the underlying logic is better motivating than any slogan.
→ Review of Week, Score Factors, Trends

How It Works

A closed loop. Each week starts with a plan. Each day feeds data back into the plan. At the end of the week, you review what happened. The system adapts. The coach intervenes only when the data says you need it.

01 Plan Set goals. Flag events. Connect to all plan types.
→
02 Check In Daily data: AI, breath, food, mood, meals.
→
03 Review End-of-week review. Weight, progress, trends.
→
04 Adapt Coach reviews. Adaption, loop adjustments.

Core Experience

01

Weekly Goals

Understanding your capacity

"Any events this week?" One question routes into five plan types — Base, Busy Week, Cheat & Rebound, Edit Plan, or Accelerate. The member sets realistic capacity for the week. The system adapts the plan to match.

Jobs To Be Done

As a member, I can...

  • • set my weekly capacity with one question so my plan matches real life.
  • • plan a cheat meal and get a rebound plan queued without judgment.
  • • start the week knowing what "on plan" looks like for me this week.
  • • skip the planning guilt — the system meets me where I am.

02

Daily Check-In

Celebrate wins and missteps

Score, mood, meals, adherence — captured daily and fed back into the weekly plan. The system treats every data point as signal, not judgment. Score increases trigger reinforcement. Drops trigger coach triage and a recovery path.

Jobs To Be Done

As a member, I can...

  • • see how today connects to what I planned this week.
  • • log my day in under 60 seconds.
  • • understand why my score changed and what to do about it.

03

Week in Review

Building motivation

Weight delta, progress bars, sparkline, activity matrix, AI summary — one scroll. The review connects effort to outcomes so the member sees what worked, not just what happened. Every number reads without explanation.

Jobs To Be Done

As a member, I can...

  • • see whether my weight moved and why, so I connect actions to results.
  • • absorb a full week of complex data in under a minute.
  • • carry momentum into next week's plan because I saw what worked.

Content Strategy

Invoy's coaching team ran everything through phone calls, spreadsheets, and manual check-ins. The workflows were effective but didn't scale. We mapped every coaching interaction, found the repeating patterns, and flattened them into UI components the system could deliver without a human in the loop.

The goal wasn't to replace coaches — it was to give them leverage. The system handles the routine; the coach steps in when the data says a member actually needs them.

02

Stakeholder Management

Three teams, one loop

The coaching team owned the workflows. The clinical team owned the health logic. Product owned the interface. Each group had different definitions of success — coaches wanted flexibility, clinical wanted compliance, product wanted simplicity.

The weekly plan framework became the shared language. Every feature decision mapped back to: does this make the loop work better? Coaches got automated triage. Clinical got standardized scoring. Product got a single interaction model that covered all five plan types.

03

Push Notifications

Contextual nudges, not interruptions

Push notifications were designed as part of the engagement loop — not a marketing channel. Every notification tied to a specific state in the weekly plan. No notification fired without a reason the member could trace back to their own data.

Plan not set by Monday

"Ready to plan your week?" — routes directly into the planning flow. Sent once. No follow-up spam.

Score drop (2+ days)

"Your coach wants to check in" — flags the coach queue and gives the member a direct path to the recovery flow.

Weekly review ready

"See how your week went" — triggers after the last check-in of the week. The review is already built; the member just opens it.

Goal streak (3+ days on plan)

"You're on a 3-day streak" — reinforcement, not a call to action. The member sees momentum without needing to do anything.

Process

01

Design Decisions

Why does one question split into 5 different flows?

Members shouldn't need to understand the system's logic. "Any events this week?" routes automatically into Base, Busy Week, Cheat & Rebound, Edit Plan, or Accelerate.

Why treat a bad day as a "rebound" instead of a failure?

Members who see a path back stay engaged. Members who feel judged stop opening the app.

Why pack so much data into the weekly review?

People look at this once a week. It has to be complete and fast to scan.

Learnings

A loop beats a list

Features that reference each other (plan → check-in → review → adapt) create engagement that standalone screens can't. The member doesn't need to remember context — the system carries it.

Recovery paths > shame

Rebound flows kept members in the system after bad days. Guilt messaging would have lost them. Designing for failure states is designing for retention.

One question can hide five flows

"Any events this week?" is simple for the member but routes into complex branching logic underneath. The best UX hides the system's complexity, not the user's options.
