Invoy

Making metabolic health coaching feel like a partnership, not a prescription. A breath sensor tells you if you're burning fat. The app turns that signal into a weekly rhythm of planning, checking in, and adapting.

Role
Designer & PM, 3-person eng team
Scope
Weekly planning flow, daily check-in redesign, push notification system, design system foundation
Timeline
2 weeks planning, 2 sprints dev — design and engineering ran in parallel

Why this Project?

The data existed. The coaching worked. Members just couldn't see it.

Invoy already had the hard pieces — a breath sensor that measured fat burn, coaches who built weekly plans, and a carb-restriction protocol that worked. But the member-facing app didn't reflect any of it. Coaches set goals behind the scenes in an internal tool. Members checked in daily without knowing what they were working toward. Roughly 30% engaged beyond breath tests. The product had depth. The interface didn't.

Framing

Invoy was a metabolic health startup with a breath sensor that could detect fat burn. 20,000 members, a clinical protocol that produced real results for members who engaged, and a coaching model that was buckling under its own weight — each coach managed 100–200 members manually. The product worked. Engagement didn't. Roughly 30% of members interacted beyond the daily breath test, and the coaching team couldn't scale without automation.

Five problems defined the project. Each was a design problem, not a science problem — the clinical model worked, but the interface was getting in the way.

No engagement rhythm beyond breath tests

One daily push notification. Members who missed a day had no reason to come back. We needed a weekly cadence — planning, check-in, mid-week boost, review — so the daily notification became one beat in a larger rhythm.

Coaching knowledge trapped in internal tools

Coaches built weekly plans through an internal tool. Members never saw any of it. The goal: surface the plan and extract enough of the coach's decision-making to suggest goals automatically.

Scientific language that didn't land

Actions stayed precise — "Less than 20g Carbs" kept its name. But the structure around them was impenetrable. "Accelerants" became "Weekly Goals." "Base plan adherence" became "Your Plan."

Clinical tone, no warmth

A medical product distributed through doctors — the voice had calcified into clinical and cold. We needed the authority of a clinical partner with the warmth of a coach who knows your name.

No design system, every screen bespoke

We carved out the weekly planning flow as the system's proving ground. Color, type, and spacing applied universally first, then components formalized from the patterns that emerged.

Experience

The weekly planning flow is the core deliverable. It takes what coaches were doing manually — reviewing a member's base plan, suggesting goals, and scheduling the week — and turns it into a 4-step flow the member completes in under 2 minutes.

01

Daily Check-in

Yesterday's actions, today's plan

The daily check-in was redesigned to reflect the weekly plan. Instead of a generic "how did you do" question, the check-in now shows the member's specific goals — their carb target, daily focus items, and weekly boosts — and asks them to mark each one. The slider for carbs replaced the old icon-tap pattern, making the input faster and more precise.

What Changed

  • • Carb target shown inline with a 3-point slider (On Plan / About / Over) — replaces the old multi-icon grid
  • • Daily Focus and Weekly Boosts appear as checkable items with completion counts
  • • "Reflect on Yesterday" framing shifts tone from grading to learning
  • • Weekly goal review link at the bottom creates a bridge back to the planning flow

02

Weekly Planning

Your week, planned around real life

The flow starts by showing the member their base plan — the carb restriction or fasting protocol their coach set up. Then it surfaces weekly goals the system recommends based on last week's data, past habits, and people with similar profiles. The member reviews, adjusts, and commits.

Design Decisions

  • • "Review Your Base Plan" makes the coach's work visible for the first time — members see what they're on and why
  • • Goals split into three tiers: Coach Recommended, Daily Focus, and Weekly Boosts — escalating commitment without overwhelming
  • • Confirmation screen shows the full week at a glance and day-by-day
  • • "Skip this week" is always available — no guilt, no friction

Content

The product loop is simple: plan your week, check in daily, review at the end. The design challenge was making each moment feel like coaching, not compliance.

Process

01

Design Decisions

Why does one question split into 5 different flows?

Members shouldn't need to understand the system's logic. "Any events this week?" routes automatically into Base, Busy Week, Cheat & Rebound, Edit Plan, or Accelerate.

Why treat a bad day as a "rebound" instead of a failure?

Members who see a path back stay engaged. Members who feel judged stop opening the app.

Why pack so much data into the weekly review?

People look at this once a week. It has to be complete and fast to scan.

Why surface the base plan to members?

Members didn't know what program they were on or what their targets were. Making the plan visible turned a black box into a partnership. Coaches could still adjust behind the scenes, but now the member understood the why.

Why build the design system inside this project?

A standalone design system initiative would have stalled. By carving out the weekly planning flow as the system's proving ground, we got both the feature and the foundation. The surrounding screens adopted the system's type and color immediately to avoid jarring transitions, then components formalized over time.

02

Design System

Started here, applied everywhere

The weekly planning flow was the seed. We built goal cards, plan summaries, and progress indicators as reusable components from day one. Then we applied the visual language — type scale, color system, spacing rhythm — to the daily check-in and home screen to create continuity. The system grew from the product, not ahead of it.
