Invoy's Weekly Plans

A weekly coaching system for weight management. Members plan their week, check in daily, review progress, and adjust — with coach support when the data says they need it.

Role
Lead product designer (Senior PM)
Company
Invoy Health
Scope
Concept Design, Feature UI, Design System
Status
Shipped to production, ~17K MAU

Why this Project?

Behavior change is the product, not the feature list.

Health coaching apps measure engagement. Invoy measured outcomes — sustained weight loss, A1C reduction, blood pressure control. The design challenge was building a lifecycle that kept people engaged long enough for the science to work. Retention was the product.

Framing

Most health apps treat each screen as a standalone moment. You check in today, but the app doesn't connect that to what you planned this week or what happened yesterday. The member has to hold the context in their head.

Invoy needed a loop, not a list of features. Weekly planning should shape daily actions. Daily data should trigger coaching. Weekly review should change next week's plan.

Motivation: Structure Direction Me
  • Decision fatigue is the enemy. Defaults to the easiest option without a clear plan. Needs to know what today looks like.
→ Week Planning, Daily Focus, Base Plan
Motivation: Support "Don't Let Me Do This Alone"
  • Knows their triggers. Struggles when unseen. Previous attempts failed not because the plan was wrong but because they were doing it alone.
→ Rebound flow, Coach triage, Score Drop CTA
Motivation: Understanding "Help Me Understand Why"
  • Asks "why" before "what." Won't follow rules they don't understand. The moment the underlying logic is better motivating than any slogan.
→ Review of Week, Score Factors, Trends

Core Experience

[screenshot]

01

Weekly Planning

Your week, planned around real life

"Any events this week?" routes into one of five plan variants. The member answers one question. The system does the branching.

Jobs to be done

  • As a member, I need my plan to account for travel and social events, so I'm not set up to fail.
  • As a member, I need lighter goals when life is busy, so I stay engaged instead of giving up.
  • As a member, I need a rebound plan activated automatically when I plan a cheat meal.
[screenshot]

02

Week in Review

See what worked without reading charts

Weight delta, progress bars, sparkline, activity matrix, AI summary — one scroll. Every number reads without explanation.

Jobs to be done

  • As a member, I need to see whether my weight moved and why, so I connect actions to results.
  • As a member, I need a summary I can absorb in under a minute, even when the data is complex.
[screenshot]

03

Updated Checkin Flow

Every day connects to the week

Daily check-ins capture score, mood, meals, adherence — then feed into the weekly loop. Score drops trigger triage. Increases trigger reinforcement.

Jobs to be done

  • As a member, I need today's check-in to connect to what I planned this week, so each day feels purposeful.
  • As a member, I need to understand why my score dropped and what to do about it.
  • As a member, I need going off plan to lead to a recovery path, not a guilt message.

Design Decisions

Why does one question split into 5 different flows?

Members shouldn't need to understand the system's logic. "Any events this week?" routes automatically into Base, Busy Week, Cheat & Rebound, Edit Plan, or Accelerate.

Why treat a bad day as a "rebound" instead of a failure?

Members who see a path back stay engaged. Members who feel judged stop opening the app.

Why pack so much data into the weekly review?

People look at this once a week. It has to be complete and fast to scan.
